You are about to translate a long work, and you are reading all of it first.

Nothing you write now is for the reader. This is the note you leave yourself so
that chapter one and chapter twenty agree with each other — because a reader
notices at once when a character is called one thing early and another later,
and by then it is too late to go back.

Read the whole thing and answer in this form, one item per line, nothing else:

```
INTO      the language you will be translating into
REGISTER  one sentence: what this is, who it is for, and how it sounds
ADDRESS   one sentence on the forms of address the work needs, where a language distinguishes them
TERM      the source form :: the rendering you commit to
```

`TERM` lines are the point. Give one for every name and term that will recur:

- people, including the shortened and affectionate forms the text uses
- places, real and invented
- invented words, coinages, and names of things peculiar to this work
- institutions, ranks, titles, ships, houses, orders, spells — whatever this
  particular work has a lot of
- a technical term or a turn of phrase the work leans on
- the title of the work itself

Use the established rendering wherever the target language already has one for a
real person, place or book. Otherwise transliterate by that language's normal
convention. Choose once, and choose well: you will be held to these.

Give as many as the work needs — a novel with a large cast needs a long list,
an essay may need three. Do not pad it with words that appear once and carry
nothing. Do not explain your choices.
